How an inductor works

An inductor is just a coil of wire. When current flows through it, it builds a magnetic field, and that field doesn't like to change. How it works

1 · A coil is lazy about change

An inductor is wire wound into a coil. Current through it makes a magnetic field (watch the compass swing). Building that field takes energy and time, and once it is built, it wants to keep going. So the coil fights any change in current: it fights the current starting, and it fights it stopping. Think of a heavy cart: hard to get rolling, and hard to stop once it rolls.

2 · Switching ON

When you turn the switch ON, the coil pushes back against the supply (the right meter swings the other way), so the current can only grow little by little. The LED fades up instead of snapping on. The bigger the coil, the slower the fade. Once the current stops changing, the coil stops pushing back, and it acts like a plain wire.

3 · Switching OFF

OFF takes the supply out but keeps the loop closed. Now the coil does the pushing: its field collapses and keeps the current flowing in the same direction, so the LED keeps glowing for a moment with no supply at all. The coil's voltage flips sign, and the energy stored in its field is used up in the resistor and the LED.

4 · Blinking and waves

If the supply blinks slowly, the coil has time to catch up and the LED blinks fully. Blink faster and the coil never has time: the current only wiggles a little around its middle, so the LED glows steadily at about half brightness. With a wave, the current lags behind the push. Coils are used like this in real circuits to smooth out bumpy currents.

For grown-ups vL = L · di/dt

The coil's voltage is its inductance times how fast the current changes. In this loop the supply's voltage is shared: vsupply = i·R + vLED + vL, with R = 220 Ω for the resistor plus 30 Ω of coil wire, and a red LED that starts to glow at 1.75 V. The current approaches its final value with time constant τ = L / Rtotal: about 63 % after τ, and essentially all of it after 5τ. Inductive reactance XL = 2πfL is why fast changes get through so poorly.
